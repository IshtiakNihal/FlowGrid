# FlowGrid

## 01. Design direction and how to use this document

**UI/UX design specification v1.0 | 26 September 2026 | BB - Noble**

FlowGrid should feel like a thoughtful interior studio with a clear understanding of family life: visually confident, warm, practical and easy to contact. The recommended direction combines architectural photography, expressive serif headlines, an orderly grid and comfortable Bangla reading. Quality comes from proportion, authentic work and carefully edited content.

This is a design proposal and handoff, not an implemented website or an approved brand identity. It covers the complete launch experience, including pages that are not pictured in the visual references. All dimensions, colors and behavior below are proposed specifications. They have not yet been validated in a working FlowGrid interface.

**The main recommendation:** use Era Residence as the strongest art-direction reference; use Thirdway for project and expertise presentation; use Quinta D. Amália selectively for spacious pacing. Adapt these principles to a Bangladeshi service business with readable Bangla and direct enquiries.

The package contains this specification, a PDF review copy, four generated reference boards, a machine-readable token file and the generation prompts. The reference boards communicate composition and atmosphere. This written specification governs exact copy, accessibility, dimensions, interaction and business claims whenever an image differs.

The generated interiors are illustrative concepts, not FlowGrid work. The proposed wordmark is a typographic placeholder until the approved FlowGrid identity is supplied. No live website, enquiry, booking or message has been created or submitted. The built-in image generator was used; its interface does not expose a selectable model version called "2.5".

**Decision status:** recommended visual direction, ready for client/design review. Final content approval and implementation testing remain separate steps.

## 04. Design principles and quality bar

1. **Show the space before describing excellence.** One strong photograph is more convincing than a row of decorative quality badges.
2. **Give each section one job.** Introduce the studio, show work, explain scope, explain process or invite an enquiry. Remove sections that repeat the same promise.
3. **Use real constraints as the story.** Storage, circulation, maintenance, family routines, natural light and an agreed scope are useful discussion topics. Confirm what applies to each project.
4. **Make essential actions visible.** A visitor should find projects, services and a way to contact the team without discovering hidden hover behavior.
5. **Keep brand expression in a few places.** Large headlines, image composition and one strong pine closing panel provide enough character.
6. **Respect local language.** Bangla text gets its own line-height and layout rules. It is not squeezed into a Latin composition.
7. **Explain commercial uncertainty honestly.** An enquiry is not a booking. A concept is not a completed project. A budget discussion is not a fixed quotation.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Repeated three-card sections, glass panels and generic bento grids | Project spreads, ruled service rows and simple editorial text/image pairings |
| Glow gradients, floating orbs, animated counters and oversized badges | Solid surfaces, deliberate whitespace and strong photographic contrast |
| Default five-star testimonials and invented achievements | Permission-cleared quotes attached to the actual service or project |
| "Luxury redefined" and similar interchangeable claims | A precise statement of what the team does and how a client begins |
| Hover-only descriptions, cursor tricks and scroll hijacking | Persistent captions, ordinary links and native scrolling |
| Image-generation interiors presented as built work | Clearly marked concept studies, separated from completed projects |

The premium result must survive removal of every animation. Evaluate the static page first. If it feels generic, improve the content, image selection or proportions before adding effects.

## 05. Audience, journeys and conversion goals

These are design hypotheses to validate with the client and representative visitors, not research findings about the entire Bangladesh market.

| Visitor situation | First question | Design response | Desired next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| A viewer arriving from Rumi's portfolio | Is this the right business, and what does it do? | Clear FlowGrid identity, interior-service explanation and modest Rumi connection | View a relevant project or service |
| A homeowner comparing studios | Can this team solve a problem like mine? | Named work, honest status, scope, decisions and genuine team roles | Open a project detail, then enquire |
| Someone planning a kitchen or one room | Do I need a full-home project? | Plain service descriptions and confirmed minimum scope | Ask about the specific space |
| A visitor concerned about cost | What changes the price, and what happens first? | Explain quotation inputs, exclusions and approval steps | Share an optional budget or choose "Not decided" |
| A busy mobile visitor | Can I speak to someone quickly? | Labelled Call and WhatsApp alternatives using verified business channels | Start a conversation |

The main conversion is a **successfully received, relevant enquiry**. Supporting measures are service-detail visits, project-detail visits and contact-link clicks. WhatsApp opens and Call clicks must remain separate from delivered leads and completed consultations.

Keep the path short: Home -> relevant project or service -> enquiry. Visitors may also arrive directly on a project through Facebook, search or a shared link. Every detail page must explain the brand and provide a next step without requiring a homepage visit.

Use four required form fields and optional project detail. Do not require an account, exact budget, floor plan or email address to start. If the business later accepts international/diaspora enquiries, add a deliberate international phone route rather than rejecting those visitors silently.

## 07. Visual identity, palette and contrast

Use deep pine as the identifying ink and action color, warm paper as the main surface, and real materials as the source of warmth. The mist surface is for an occasional process or next-step panel. Clay is a small secondary accent, not a second competing action color.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `color.surface.page` | `#F4F1E8` | Default background |
| `color.surface.clean` | `#FFFFFF` | Form surface or inverse text where needed |
| `color.surface.mist` | `#DEE7E2` | Supporting panel; use sparingly |
| `color.text.primary` | `#183B35` | Headings, body and essential icons |
| `color.text.secondary` | `#56645E` | Supporting copy and metadata |
| `color.action.primary` | `#183B35` | Main button background |

| `color.accent.clay` | `#895239` | Small emphasis and selected editorial detail |
| `color.border.decorative` | `#B8C2BA` | Nonessential section dividers |
| `color.border.control` | `#718178` | Input and outlined-control boundaries |
| `color.focus` | `#895239` | Outer focus ring on light surfaces |
| `color.status.error` | `#9B302B` | Error text and icon; always paired with words |
| `color.status.success` | `#245C43` | Received-state text/icon; never the only signal |

Calculated WCAG contrast ratios for exact solid token pairs are included in the token file and PDF palette sheet. Check the implemented combinations again, especially alpha colors and image overlays. Ordinary text needs at least 4.5:1; qualifying large text needs 3:1 [S9]. Essential control boundaries need sufficient non-text contrast; decorative separators are not input borders.

Do not use bright WhatsApp green with white text. Render the label "WhatsApp" in the normal pine system, with an optional recognizable outline icon. Do not change the palette simply to imitate a platform button.

Use a 2px outer focus ring with a 2px paper gap. On dark surfaces, use a paper outer ring with a dark separation gap. Focus must remain visible around the entire control. Default to flat surfaces: shadows are reserved for an overlay where necessary to separate layers, not project cards.

## 09. Grid, spacing and responsive rules

| Viewport | Grid | Outer gutter | Column gap | Typical section space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 320-767px | 4 columns; most content full width | 20px, 16px at 320px if needed | 16px | 56-72px |
| 768-1023px | 8 columns | 32px | 24px | 80-96px |
| 1024-1439px | 12 columns | 48px | 24px | 96-120px |
| 1440px and above | 12 columns, max content 1312px | At least 64px | 24px | 112-144px |

Spacing tokens: **4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 128px**. Use 8px multiples for major composition; 4px adjustments for fine alignment. Radius: 0px for media, 2px for controls, 4px maximum for an exceptional overlay. No default pill buttons.

At 1440px with 64px gutters and eleven 24px gaps, the 1312px content region yields twelve approximately 87.3px columns. Use 7/5 or 7/4-plus-one-empty-column compositions for feature spreads. Project listing rhythm may alternate 7/5 and 5/7; keep reading order logical.

Headers: 88px desktop, 72px mobile, allowed to grow with text zoom. Use a solid surface. A sticky header must not obscure anchors or keyboard focus. At 1024px, use the compact menu if the chosen translation does not fit with at least 24px between navigation groups.

Mobile rules: stack images and copy; remove asymmetrical vertical offsets; put title and primary action on paper before the hero image; use a 4:5 image crop; do not force a full-screen hero height. Project cards use a 4:3 image and visible caption. Form fields are always one column. Two-column facts collapse into a definition list.

The two-action mobile contact bar appears on content pages after the hero leaves view. Reserve its height plus safe-area inset; hide it while a dialog/menu is open, while the keyboard is active, and on Contact. It must not cover focused content. A visible ordinary Contact link remains available if the bar cannot be implemented reliably.

## 12. Services and process

The proposed initial service categories are **Home interiors**, **Kitchens & storage**, and **Renovation planning**. Confirm the actual offer before publishing. Commercial interiors, turnkey construction, procurement, civil work, structural changes and full project management must not be implied just because they are common industry categories.

Each service row has a title, one useful sentence and a visible destination. For a service detail, provide the typical problem, included deliverables, exclusions, what the client provides, illustrative/real relevant work, cost drivers and a contextual enquiry action. Do not create thin pages that repeat the same marketing copy with different titles.

| Proposed process stage | Explain to the visitor | Required owner clarification |
|---|---|---|

## 16. Motion specification and linked-skill decisions

The linked repositories were reviewed as design guidance. No packages were installed into a website and no website was built. Emil Kowalski's design-engineering and animate guidance supplies the primary decision framework: establish a purpose, then choose economical motion with suitable timing [S6]. iart-ai's relevant branch is its web-animation collection rather than its video/3D packs [S7]. Delphi's examples are useful secondary references, especially for state transitions [S8].

There is a concrete disagreement: Delphi's quick table suggests ease-in for exits, while Emil's guidance rejects ease-in for UI transitions. For FlowGrid, use the specified ease-out curve for short enters/exits, with faster exits. Do not combine conflicting recipes automatically.

| Interaction | Proposed timing | Treatment | Reduced-motion mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Button hover | 150ms, ease | Small color change; no lift | Immediate color state |
| Pointer press | 100ms, ease-out | At most scale 0.99 on button surface | No scale |
| Menu | 220ms enter, 160ms exit | Opacity and up to 8px translation; interaction ready immediately | Instant open/close |
| FAQ | 180ms | Discreet reveal; prevent jumps where feasible | Instant expansion |
| Project hover | 240ms | Optional image scale 1.015 inside fixed crop; caption stays visible | No scale |
| Below-fold image reveal | 360ms once | At most 12px translation plus opacity; never hold readable content invisible | Show final state immediately |
| Gallery change | 180ms | Brief opacity transition after user action | Immediate replacement |
| Form state | 150ms | Replace status text; preserve layout | Immediate state |

Motion curve: `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` for the specified ease-out. No looping marquee, parallax, cursor follower, forced loader, letter-by-letter Bangla reveal, animated statistics or timed page-entry curtain. Keyboard-initiated state changes should be immediate. Hover effects apply only when a pointer actually supports hover.

Use ordinary scrolling. Content must be visible if enhancement fails. Prefer opacity and transforms for decorative motion, but do not claim those properties alone guarantee smoothness; profile the implemented behavior. Avoid a motion library solely for a fade. Honor reduced-motion preferences throughout. WCAG interaction-animation guidance is a useful enhanced accessibility reference; its cited criterion is AAA, not an AA requirement [S11].

## 17. Photography and image-generation boundaries

The photographic direction is warm daylight, believable urban proportions and carefully observed details. Use a few strong wide views, medium compositions that explain a layout, and close details that prove material quality. Include everyday objects sparingly so rooms feel usable. Avoid excessive HDR, identical beige rooms, impossible openings and unnaturally flawless materials.

Bangladesh relevance should come from the actual projects, people and living context. Do not insert token cultural props or assume every family has the same preferences. Discuss storage, maintenance, privacy, kitchen routines and household needs with real clients where relevant. The generated boards use wood, cane and screened light as visual exploration, not as claims about FlowGrid's material standard.

| Asset | Preferred source / capture |
|---|---|
| Hero | One strongest approved completed project; landscape master with a deliberate mobile crop |
| Project story | 8-12 useful photographs per substantial case study, each with a purpose |
| Detail photos | Joinery, finish transitions, storage and real workmanship |
| Process | Genuine site review, drawings or material discussion, with permission |
| Studio | Real people and accurate names/roles; no generated team portraits |
| Before / after | Same space, comparable angles and truthful dates |
| Concept | Original design study, visible concept label, kept distinct from completed work |

Image ratios: hero 16:9 or 1.8:1 desktop and 4:5 mobile; project index 4:3; portrait detail 4:5; supporting banner 2:1. Store a mobile focal point and caption. Do not crop out the feature explained by the text.

For every asset record the source, owner, permission, photographer, project status, caption, alternative text, crop and any privacy restrictions. Do not publish identifiable plans, exact residential addresses, family photographs or client possessions without appropriate permission.

Generated boards are creative references only. They must not be repurposed as testimonials, build evidence, before/after proof or technical construction drawings. The written specification takes precedence over raster text and minor image inconsistencies.

## 18. Accessibility, performance and browser behavior

Target WCAG 2.2 AA in implementation. The following checks support that target; passing this short list alone is not a conformance claim. Use real semantic headings, navigation landmarks, buttons for actions, links for destinations and associated field labels. Provide a skip link and meaningful page titles.

Ordinary text contrast must meet 4.5:1, with the standard large-text exception [S9]. FlowGrid's design target is at least 48px for principal touch controls, exceeding WCAG 2.2 AA's 24px minimum target-size criterion with its defined exceptions [S10]. Test text zoom at 200% and reflow at 320 CSS pixels. No clipped Bengali conjuncts, lost buttons or horizontal page scrolling.

Check keyboard navigation, visible focus, sensible order, dialog focus containment/return, gallery controls, disclosures and form-error announcements. When a fixed header or contact bar is present, focused items must stay visible. Do not depend on motion, drag, color or hover alone to communicate meaning.

Performance acceptance targets are LCP <=2.5 seconds, INP <=200ms and CLS <=0.1 at the 75th percentile in field data [S15]. These are targets, not achieved FlowGrid measurements. Lighthouse is useful diagnostic evidence, not a substitute for real visits.

Proposed initial budgets: mobile hero <=250KB, desktop hero <=400KB, initial viewport transfer around <=1MB, initial JavaScript around <=150KB compressed, and initial fonts around <=200KB compressed for the selected language. These are working budgets to test against visual quality and real font subsets, not universal requirements. Revise explicitly when evidence justifies it.

Use responsive images with reserved dimensions, load the hero promptly and lazy-load lower images. Deliver suitable modern formats with fallbacks. Load the requested language's font assets; preserve Bengali shaping tables when subsetting. Do not preload unused styles. Use posters with intentional video playback, no autoplay audio and no eager third-party players/maps.

Test current Android Chrome, iPhone Safari, desktop Chrome/Firefox/Safari, and Facebook/YouTube in-app browsing where available. Verify deep links, Back navigation, tel/WhatsApp fallbacks, slow connections and failed submissions. Reference-site behavior is not evidence that FlowGrid will behave correctly.

## 19. Content model and ownership

Design content as structured records so updates do not require changing layout. This is a content contract, not a recommendation for a specific CMS or framework.

| Record | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Brand | Approved spelling, logo files, language names, service-area statement, cross-site destinations |
| Contact | Channel type, verified address/number, owner, availability statement if confirmed, last review date |
| Project | Stable identifier, translated title/slug, status, scope, area basis, general location, brief, decisions, gallery, credits, permission |
| Service | Name, summary, inclusions, exclusions, deliverables, client inputs, related projects, enquiry context |
| Person | Name, public role, biography, approved portrait, verified credentials if displayed |
| Quote | Exact words, speaker/approved attribution, associated service, source, reuse permission |
| Media | File, dimensions, focal point, alt text per language, caption, owner, permission, concept/built status |
| Policy | Owner, effective date, reviewed content, related workflow |

The business owner approves factual and commercial content. The designer owns visual consistency and content fit. The developer owns working interactions, submission evidence and accessibility/performance testing. A named Bangla reviewer checks language. One person should own changing contact details across the site.

Maintain a private content register with source and approval status. Unsupported claims stay out of public pages. Do not infer follower counts as customer numbers or present a concept as an executed job. If a field has no verified value, omit it gracefully rather than printing a dash across a supposedly complete case study.

Optional analytics events: project opened, service opened, enquiry started, enquiry validation failed, enquiry received, contact channel clicked. Never put names, phone numbers, budget text or message content in analytics events or URLs. Distinguish receipt from a simple button click. Search metadata should describe FlowGrid's actual interior service and use the final approved domain, not a preview URL.
